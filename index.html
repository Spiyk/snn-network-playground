Spiking Network: float vs 8-bit fixed-point

50 leaky integrate-and-fire neurons with random connections, driven by random input spikes. The same network runs twice on identical input: once with ordinary decimal numbers, once with small integers like a hardware chip would use. Change the number of bits and watch how the behaviour changes.

What to try

  • With defaults, both networks fire at similar rates, but the exact spike times drift apart after a few steps.
  • Lower the weight bits to 3 or 4. Weights get rounded to coarse values, and the fixed-point network can behave very differently.
  • Set membrane bits to 8 and raise the inhibitory weight. The potential hits its limits and the saturation counter climbs.
  • Rates are a better comparison than exact spike times, because small rounding differences grow in a recurrent network.

This is a teaching model with no learning in it. It is not an accurate copy of any specific chip.
